== Overview of the ESPD Parts

The ESPD is structured into six parts. The first part covers procedure identification, parts 2 through 5 cover the substantive qualification content, and part 6 contains legal declarations.

Each part is provided by the buyer, the economic operator, or both, depending on the ESPD document type (Request or Response) and the role of the participant.

== Part 1: Procedure Information

*Buyer provides:* Information concerning the procurement procedure and the buyer — the publication reference (Contract Notice), the identity of the buyer, and the procurement procedure details.

*Economic operator provides:* Information identifying the ESPD Request to which it is responding, by referencing the publication, the identity of the buyer, and the procurement procedure.

== Part 2: Economic Operator Information

NOTE: The buyer does not provide this section.

*The economic operator provides:*

* Information about the economic operator (identity, size, registration status, group participation);
* Information about representatives of the economic operator;
* Information about reliance on the capacities of other entities;
* Information concerning subcontractors on whose capacity the economic operator does not rely.

== Part 3: Exclusion Grounds

*Buyer provides:* The exclusion grounds that apply to the procedure:

* *Part 3A* — Grounds relating to criminal convictions (Article 57(1) of Directive 2014/24/EU) — mandatory;
* *Part 3B* — Grounds relating to the payment of taxes or social security contributions (Article 57(2)) — mandatory;
* *Part 3C* — Grounds relating to insolvency, conflicts of interests or professional misconduct (Article 57(4)) — discretionary, buyer selects which apply;
* *Part 3D* — Purely national exclusion grounds — depend on the regulation of each country, buyer selects which apply.

*Economic operator provides:* A response to all applicable exclusion grounds, declaring compliance and providing explanations where relevant.

NOTE: The economic operator must respond to all exclusion grounds selected by the buyer.

== Part 4: Selection Criteria

*Buyer provides:* The selection criteria that apply, organised by category:

* *Part 4A* — Suitability (Article 58(2));
* *Part 4B* — Economic and financial standing (Article 58(3));
* *Part 4C* — Technical and professional ability (Article 58(4));
* *Part 4D* — Quality assurance schemes and environmental management standards (Article 62(2)).

The buyer may also specify requirements and thresholds for each criterion, and associate criteria with specific lots.

*Economic operator provides:* A response to each applicable selection criterion, with the required information and evidence references.

== Part 5: Reduction of Qualified Candidates

NOTE: The buyer does not provide this section.

*The economic operator provides:* Information where the buyer has specified objective and non-discriminatory criteria or rules to limit the number of candidates invited to tender or to participate in a dialogue within a two-phased procedure.

This part does not apply when the economic operator is a subcontractor that the lead entity does not rely on in order to meet the selection criteria.

== Part 6: Concluding Statements

*The economic operator declares that:*

* The information submitted under Parts 2–5 is accurate and true;
* It is able to provide certificates and/or other forms of documentary evidence listed;
* It consents to grant the buyer access to where the documentary evidence is stored.

=== Key rules

* Exclusion grounds apply to *all lots* automatically.
* Selection criteria apply *per lot* and may differ between lots.
* Each participating party provides *one ESPD Response per lot* (or group of lots) they tender for.
* It is the *Group Leader's responsibility* to collect the completed ESPD Responses from all group members and submit them with the tender.
